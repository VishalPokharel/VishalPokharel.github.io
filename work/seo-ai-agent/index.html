Finding the right customer for an AI SEO teammate.

I am building an AI teammate for local SEO agencies. It learns the agency’s process, performs repetitive execution, and prepares evidence for human review.

Role: founder and builder / Status: active / ICP: local SEO agencies with established teams and recurring client workflows.

Context

What changed

I started with the idea of an autonomous SEO employee. The more I worked with real clients and agencies, the more that framing became too broad. SEO work has process, judgment, review, and client context. A useful product needs to sit inside that workflow rather than pretend the workflow does not exist.

The current direction is narrower: an assistant for local SEO teams that researches competitors, collects evidence, prepares repetitive work, and leaves important decisions to humans.

Some names, revenue figures, and agency details still need permission before they should be published publicly.

Pivot timeline

Belief, action, result, change

SaaS founders

I began with a founder profile similar to the people I already understood. I filtered Product Hunt launches and sent around 200 cold emails. Only two replied. The lesson was simple: matching a profile does not mean the person has buying intent.

Communities with clearer intent

I moved toward X, Reddit, and Facebook groups. The first paying SaaS customer came from this phase: a $40 trial followed by $100. That proved someone outside my network would pay, but it did not prove the segment was right.

General SEO service work

I took on e-commerce and other service clients to learn delivery. This made the dependencies visible: developers, client teams, unclear ownership, and work that repeated across accounts.

Direct local businesses

I tested dental clinics, skincare clinics, physiotherapy, psychiatry, tourism, education, and other local service businesses through cold calling and direct outreach. Business owners wanted outcomes, but many did not want another dashboard to use.

Self-serve product

We built a dashboard with progress tracking, review workflows, posts, and AI replies. People did not use it enough. That failure changed the product from “give owners a tool” to “help the people already doing the work.”

Personalized WhatsApp and Loom outreach

I tested custom videos and WhatsApp messages for computer repair shops and DIY users across Nepal, Chennai, and Bengaluru. The response was stronger than generic cold email, but the exact conversion metric needs clarification before it belongs on the public page.

Agency model, freelancers, and established agencies

Operating like an AI-native agency helped reveal the work. Freelancer conversations showed budget and workflow differences. Established local SEO agencies became the stronger direction because they already have recurring client work, review habits, and a reason to save execution time.

What I am testing now

Current questions

  • Can competitor discovery be reliable enough for agency review?
  • Can agencies review output quickly?
  • Can corrections improve future runs?
  • Does the product save meaningful time on a recurring workflow?
  • Will agencies pay for a focused assistant instead of a broad autonomous employee?
